How much of a sequence repeats

Each cell is how cheaply frame j is predicted from frame i when both are coded together (appearance residual plus Bures–Wasserstein covariance distance), normalised per sequence; bright is similar. ρ is the mean cost between frames more than 150 apart over the mean cost between neighbours: above 1 means temporal structure.

How the numbers are made

Measurement protocol

Identical-source PSNR

The reference is a render of the uncompressed input Gaussian frame from every calibrated N3DV camera. Squared error is pooled over all pixels, views and 300 frames before conversion to dB, in RGB and in BT.709 YUV. It isolates coding loss from the generator's own reconstruction error, so curves of different generators do not share a scale.

Per-frame mean PSNR and SSIM

The D-FCGS and D-GPCC pipelines report PSNR averaged over frames in dB, which is lower than the pooled value. Choose “PSNR · RGB (per-frame mean)” to put static and dynamic codecs on one axis.

Ground truth

PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS (AlexNet) against the captured images of the held-out camera. Here every generator shares one reference, and published results can be placed beside ours; both arms saturate at the generator's ceiling, so BD-rates on this axis are indicative at best.

Rate and BD-rate

MB per frame is total bytes ÷ frames ÷ 106, including every I-frame. BD-rate fits log rate as a cubic in quality (quadratic with three points) and integrates both fits over the quality range the curves share.

Six-scene average

The i-th rate point of each scene is averaged across the six scenes, and BD-rate is taken on the averaged curves. An average appears only when all six scenes carry the same rate ladder.

Provenance

Every point is a row of results/tables in the repository (commit ), traced to its raw score file in results/PROVENANCE.csv. Decoded frames and bitstreams were not kept.
